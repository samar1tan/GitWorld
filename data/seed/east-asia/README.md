# East Asia source-based history collection

This ordinary KnowledgeCollection bundle contains **1,214 objects, 4,031 claims and 22 source records**. Eleven source records contain selected facts from fixed English Wikipedia revisions. Three preserve the original interpretation, chronology and display-translation evidence; eight preserve inspectable mapping plans and their derivations. All claims remain unreviewed. A row count is not historical completeness or installed-application acceptance.

The base collection covers Chinese rulership surveys, Japanese imperial and shogunal offices, modern executive-office surveys, competing ancient chronologies and twenty source-listed Japan–Tang mission entries. Northern Yuan, Southern Ming and Later Jin/Qing supplement the main lists. Period definitions, offices, courts and political subjects do not become one timeless country.

## Additional structure

The activity-context mapping adds 70 unreviewed links from 35 evidenced joins. It relates source-listed senders/counterparts to declared offices or political contexts at the recorded time, with inverse links for exploration. Every link retains both supporting claims, fixed source revisions and conservative possible applicability. It does not establish that a mission occurred, completed contact, transmitted an institution or created ancestry. Five unresolved source aliases remain unresolved.

The qualified-office mapping adds 15 unreviewed claims. It pairs table endpoints only within the same named holder cell and an explicit authority basis. Source-listed de jure holders and de facto authority are separate fields. For example, the source can retain multiple effective-authority candidates; the mapping does not decide which person truly controlled government. Missing endpoints and unqualified cells remain exceptions. The older generic office-holder projection has not been silently rewritten.

## Meaning and limitations

Every installed object has English and Simplified Chinese display labels. Source originals remain intact; translations and modeling decisions have separate provenance. Possible temporal applicability is not a certain tenure. First/last observations do not establish an object's origin or extinction, and a source's geometry interval is not its political lifetime. No coordinates, political ancestry, voluntary mergers or institutional influence are invented to fill a view.

This remains a research foundation. Archaeological coverage, important parallel regimes, sourced political transformations, constitutional change and cultural transmission need further evidence. The [case inventory](../../../docs/acceptance/china-japan-coverage.md) distinguishes mapped coverage and gaps. Published source access does not itself approve a new distribution.

## Evidenced political and institutional changes

The political-integration extension records seven unreviewed claims linking Eastern Wu and Western Jin to the source-stated conquest and incorporation outcome in 280. Campaign date uncertainty remains explicit. These typed participant links do not fabricate Git ancestry or describe the conquest as a voluntary merger.

The constitutional-states extension records nineteen unreviewed claims about the Japanese imperial office, two constitutional texts, their promulgation and effectivity, and the formally assigned roles of the Cabinet and Diet. The officeholder and constitutional role are separate fields. The comparison concerns normative rules, not proof of actual political control. The post-1947 validity evidence is capped at February 2006; unknown later coverage is not repeal.

Each extension has a separate plan, bilingual field catalog and rights record under `extensions/`. The constitutional selected-output review binds exact source hashes, fifteen factual fields, original model/presentation hashes and policy evidence. It covers only those selected facts and original interpretations. The original constitutional translations, whitepaper prose, HTML, images and PDFs are excluded; uncertain raw-source rights remain unknown. The supplied CC BY-SA 4.0 grant does not claim exclusive ownership of bare legal facts. Attribution to NDL, MEXT and the Law Library of Congress and the cited compatible-use bases remain in the rights record.

These additions do not establish complete China/Japan coverage, a working source-acquisition interface for these profiles, installed-application acceptance or release approval.

## Connected transitions and coinage evidence

The political-transition extension adds twenty unreviewed claims. It connects the 263 Wei conquest of Shu, the 266 Wei–Jin dynastic replacement and the existing 280 Jin–Wu integration through explicit participants, distinct mechanisms and outcomes. These are separate transformations; common comparison fields use their source-stated year, while finer source dates and uncertainty remain in the evidence. The seven earlier 280 claims are preserved byte for byte. No Git parentage or automatic political inheritance follows from these relations.

The coinage extension adds fifteen unreviewed claims around two factual references from the Bank of Japan Currency Museum: the 708 Wadō issue and evidence of earlier Fuhon-sen production. The recipient, coin model and Tang context have separate typed fields and bilingual presentation. The records do not name a specific carrier or assert a completed mission or wholesale institutional transfer. Before and after the dated issue, unsupported issuer states remain unknown; this is not a claim that coinage began or ended at a query boundary. Earlier production retains possible interval timing and an unidentified issuer.

The coinage distribution classification binds the two fact-record hashes and GitWorld's original typed objects, statements and presentation. It does not include museum paragraphs, translations, HTML, images or the page compilation. The acquired work and ordinary normalization retain unknown redistribution rights; the separate classification neither supplies BOJ/IMES permission nor changes historical review. See the [exact scope and policy evidence](extensions/coinage-transmission/selected-output-scope.json). Both extensions include a paired `import.json` using the ordinary collection-and-vocabulary format.

## Period definitions and dated institutional observations

Six source-specific archaeological definitions retain 32 unreviewed claims from fixed English Wikipedia editions: Yangshao, Longshan, Liangzhu, Jōmon, Yayoi and Kofun. Eight extents preserve Yayoi's conventional and earlier chronology alternatives. Approximate dates remain possible, ordinal centuries retain complete civil-year envelopes, and the source calendar remains unspecified. These definitions establish neither ancient states nor civilization origins. Their boundary time values remain separate from the definition's temporal applicability. Outside a supplied extent, applicability is unknown.

The court/office/constitutional-text extension adds 29 unreviewed claims on six new subjects and six unchanged existing subjects. The 1392 settlement concerns imperial claims and an undertaking whose reported 1412 breach remains qualified; it is not a nation merger or legitimacy judgment. The 1885 office replacement and separately cited Cabinet formation share a date without backdating the later Cabinet role. Four Chinese constitutional texts retain observations at their stated adoption dates. Source-listed head-of-state provisions and premier nomination are separate fields. A None table entry is not an original-law audit; explicit abolition/restoration use independent cited passages and distinct offices. Adoption observations do not establish continuous enforcement.

Both extensions include ordinary paired collection/vocabulary `import.json` files. Fixed-edition identifiers, source hashes, author-history links, selection guards and changes remain in their attribution/plan files. Raw HTML, paragraphs, anchor labels and images are excluded.

## Research-context navigation

The initial China-related and Japan-related research contexts organize the actual source material through explicit selectors and typed links. Their 921 and 351 members overlap where the sources connect; all 1,214 objects are assigned to at least one context. These are editable research groupings, not two timeless country objects. Users can create other contexts and assign whole saved-source or adopted batches through the same workbench workflow.

The navigation plan records all selected source revisions/hashes, explicit definition/observation subjects and declared chronology links. Twenty-three type roles organize structures, people, source groups, definitions and documents without changing historical object types or original claims. The optional bootstrap is hash-checked with the manifest and initializes only an empty repository. Existing libraries and restored backups are retained. User organization changes enter ordinary working changes, staging and commits; they do not alter source receipts or historical review status.
